## 🗓️ Daily System (60--90 minutes)

``` text
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ DAILY SPANISH LOOP                         │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 15 min  Duolingo Pro lesson + review        │
│ 15 min  Vocabulary / spaced repetition      │
│ 20 min  Listening (audio + transcript)      │
│ 15 min  Speaking aloud / shadowing          │
│ 10 min  Grammar or writing                  │
│  5 min  Recall yesterday without notes      │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**Minimum viable day (20 min):** 10 min Duolingo + 5 min listening + 5
min speaking aloud.

**Weekly rhythm** - **Mon--Fri:** Follow daily loop. - **Saturday:**
Review weak skills; write a 150--250 word piece (scale to level). -
**Sunday:** Light Spanish media + weekly self-test; plan next week's
target.

### Duolingo Pro workflow

1.  Complete the next lesson, but say every sentence aloud.
2.  Before tapping an answer, predict it yourself.
3.  Save *useful* new words/phrases in a personal notebook (avoid
    collecting every word).
4.  Review mistakes: write the corrected sentence and the reason.
5.  Revisit the same unit after a few days and test recall without
    hints.
6.  Use Duolingo's speaking/listening exercises when available; add real
    conversation elsewhere.

**Do not measure mastery by streak, XP, or unit completion alone.**
Measure what you can understand and produce from memory.

# 1. 🔊 Pronunciation & Reading (A0 → A1)

Spanish spelling is relatively consistent. Learn the sound system first.

  Letter / pattern   Approximate sound                       Example
  ------------------ --------------------------------------- --------------
  a, e, i, o, u      ah, eh, ee, oh, oo                      casa, mesa
  h                  silent                                  hola
  j; ge/gi           strong breathy "h" (varies by region)   jamón, gente
  ñ                  "ny"                                    niño
  ll, y              often "y" (regional variation)          llave, yo
  r                  tap between vowels / at onset           pero
  rr; initial r      rolled/trilled r                        perro, rojo
  c + e/i (Spain)    "th"; Latin America usually "s"         cinco
  z (Spain)          "th"; Latin America usually "s"         zapato
  qu + e/i           k sound; u silent                       queso
  güe/güi            pronounce the u                         pingüino

### Stress rules

``` text
Word ends in vowel, n, or s  → stress usually penultimate syllable
Word ends in other consonant → stress usually final syllable
Written accent (á, é, í, ó, ú) → marks stress or distinguishes meaning
```

Examples: **casa**, **hablan**, **reloj**, **café**, **inglés**,
**está**.

### Accent marks are meaningful

-   **sí** = yes; **si** = if
-   **tú** = you; **tu** = your
-   **él** = he; **el** = the
-   **más** = more; **mas** = but (literary)
-   **¿...? / ¡...!**: Spanish uses opening and closing punctuation.

### First speaking drill

Read slowly, then naturally: - Hola. Me llamo \_\_\_. - Soy de India. -
Estoy aprendiendo español. - ¿Cómo estás? Estoy bien, gracias. - Mucho
gusto. ¡Hasta luego!

# 2. 🧱 A1 Foundations

## Essential sentence architecture

``` text
SUBJECT + VERB + COMPLEMENT
Yo       estudio  español.
I        study    Spanish.

QUESTION WORD + VERB + SUBJECT / COMPLEMENT
¿Dónde    vives?
Where     do you live?
```

Spanish often omits subject pronouns because the verb ending identifies
the subject: - **(Yo) hablo español.** - Use **yo** for emphasis or
contrast: *Yo hablo; él no.*

## Subject pronouns

  Spanish                   English
  ------------------------- --------------------------------------------
  yo                        I
  tú                        you (informal singular)
  él / ella / usted         he / she / you formal
  nosotros/as               we
  vosotros/as               you plural (mainly Spain)
  ellos / ellas / ustedes   they / you plural (Latin America: ustedes)

## Articles & gender

-   **el** libro (masculine), **la** casa (feminine)
-   **los** libros, **las** casas
-   **un** libro, **una** casa
-   Adjectives agree: *un chico alto; una chica alta; chicos altos;
    chicas altas.*
-   Gender is grammatical; learn nouns with their article: **la mesa**,
    not just *mesa*.

## SER vs ESTAR vs HAY

``` text
SER     identity, origin, inherent description, time
        Soy estudiante. Somos de India. Es lunes.

ESTAR   state, condition, location
        Estoy cansado. El libro está aquí.

HAY     there is / there are (existence)
        Hay una biblioteca. Hay dos libros.
```

A useful starter contrast: - **Es aburrido.** = It/he is boring
(characteristic). - **Está aburrido.** = It/he is bored (state). - **Es
listo.** = smart. - **Está listo.** = ready.

## Present tense: regular verbs

  Infinitive      -AR: hablar   -ER: comer   -IR: vivir
  --------------- ------------- ------------ ------------
  yo              hablo         como         vivo
  tú              hablas        comes        vives
  él/ella/usted   habla         come         vive
  nosotros/as     hablamos      comemos      vivimos
  vosotros/as     habláis       coméis       vivís
  ellos/ustedes   hablan        comen        viven

### High-frequency irregulars --- learn these early

  Verb     yo       tú        él/ella   nosotros   ellos/ustedes
  -------- -------- --------- --------- ---------- ---------------
  ser      soy      eres      es        somos      son
  estar    estoy    estás     está      estamos    están
  tener    tengo    tienes    tiene     tenemos    tienen
  ir       voy      vas       va        vamos      van
  hacer    hago     haces     hace      hacemos    hacen
  decir    digo     dices     dice      decimos    dicen
  poder    puedo    puedes    puede     podemos    pueden
  querer   quiero   quieres   quiere    queremos   quieren
  venir    vengo    vienes    viene     venimos    vienen
  saber    sé       sabes     sabe      sabemos    saben

## Stem-changing verbs

``` text
e → ie : querer → quiero, quieres, quiere ... queremos
o → ue : poder  → puedo, puedes, puede ... podemos
e → i  : pedir  → pido, pides, pide ... pedimos
```

Usually the change does not happen in **nosotros** (and **vosotros**).

## Negation & questions

-   **No** before verb: *No entiendo.*
-   Yes/no question: *¿Hablas español?*
-   Question words: **qué** (what), **quién** (who), **cuándo** (when),
    **dónde** (where), **por qué** (why), **cómo** (how),
    **cuánto/a/os/as** (how much/many), **cuál/es** (which).
-   **¿Por qué?** = why; **porque** = because.

## A1 survival vocabulary buckets

-   Greetings: hola, buenos días, buenas tardes, buenas noches, adiós,
    hasta mañana.
-   Courtesy: por favor, gracias, de nada, perdón, disculpe.
-   People: persona, amigo/a, familia, hombre, mujer, niño/a.
-   Places: casa, escuela, tienda, estación, baño, ciudad.
-   Food: agua, pan, arroz, fruta, café, leche, pollo.
-   Numbers, days, months, time, colors, directions.
-   Core verbs: ser, estar, tener, hacer, ir, querer, poder, hablar,
    comer, vivir, necesitar.

**A1 output mission:** introduce yourself, order food, ask directions,
describe family/home, tell time, and handle simple transactions.

# 3. 🕰️ A2: Everyday Life & Past Events

## Past tense map

``` text
PRESENT ───────────────► now / habit
PRETÉRITO ────────────► completed event / bounded action
IMPERFECTO ───────────► background, habit, ongoing past
PERFECTO COMPUESTO ──► past linked to present (usage varies by region)
```

### Pretérito (simple past) regular endings

  Person          -AR       -ER/-IR
  --------------- --------- ---------
  yo              -é        -í
  tú              -aste     -iste
  él/ella/usted   -ó        -ió
  nosotros/as     -amos     -imos
  vosotros/as     -asteis   -isteis
  ellos/ustedes   -aron     -ieron

Examples: *Ayer estudié. Comimos. Vivieron allí.*

Common irregular stems: **tener → tuv-**, **estar → estuv-**, **hacer →
hic-** (hizo), **ir/ser → fui**, **poder → pud-**, **poner → pus-**,
**decir → dij-**.

### Imperfecto endings

  Person          -AR       -ER/-IR
  --------------- --------- ---------
  yo              -aba      -ía
  tú              -abas     -ías
  él/ella/usted   -aba      -ía
  nosotros/as     -ábamos   -íamos
  vosotros/as     -abais    -íais
  ellos/ustedes   -aban     -ían

Only three major irregulars: **ser → era**, **ir → iba**, **ver →
veía**.

**Contrast** - *Ayer llovió.* = It rained (event). - *Cuando era niño,
llovía mucho.* = It used to rain a lot (background/habit). - *Estudiaba
cuando llamaste.* = I was studying when you called.

## Reflexive verbs

-   *Me levanto a las siete.* = I get up at seven.
-   *te / se / nos / os / se*
-   Daily routine: levantarse, ducharse, vestirse, acostarse,
    despertarse.

## Gustar-style verbs

``` text
Me gusta el café.       I like coffee.
Me gustan los libros.   I like books.
Me gusta estudiar.      I like studying.
```

The verb agrees with the thing liked: **gusta** (singular/infinitive),
**gustan** (plural).

Other similar verbs: encantar, interesar, importar, molestar, faltar.

## Object pronouns

-   Direct: **lo/la/los/las** (it/him/her/them as direct object)
-   Indirect: **me/te/le/nos/os/les** (to/for someone)
-   Example: *Veo el libro → Lo veo.* / *Doy el libro a Ana → Le doy el
    libro.*
-   Combined: *Se lo doy.* (**le/les** becomes **se** before
    lo/la/los/las.)

## Comparisons

-   más ... que = more ... than
-   menos ... que = less ... than
-   tan ... como = as ... as
-   mejor / peor = better / worse
-   mayor / menor = older / younger (often age)

**A2 output mission:** explain your routine, describe a past weekend,
talk about plans, shop, travel, and narrate a simple story.

# 4. 🚀 B1: Independent Spanish

## Future & conditional

-   Simple future: infinitive + **é, ás, á, emos, éis, án**\
    *Mañana estudiaré.*
-   Near future: **ir a + infinitive**\
    *Voy a estudiar.*
-   Conditional: infinitive + **ía, ías, ía, íamos, íais, ían**\
    *Me gustaría viajar.*
-   Irregular stems: **dir-** (decir), **har-** (hacer), **tendr-**
    (tener), **podr-** (poder), **querr-** (querer), **vendr-** (venir),
    **saldr-** (salir).

## Present perfect

**haber + past participle** - he, has, ha, hemos, habéis, han +
hablado/comido/vivido - *He terminado.* = I have finished. - Irregular
participles: hecho, dicho, visto, escrito, puesto, vuelto, abierto,
muerto. - Regional usage differs: many Latin American speakers commonly
use the simple preterite where some Spain speakers use the present
perfect.

## Commands (starter)

-   **tú affirmative:** habla, come, vive; irregular: di, haz, ve, pon,
    sal, sé, ten, ven.
-   Negative commands use subjunctive: *No hables. No comas.*
-   Polite: *Hable, por favor.*

## Subjunctive --- the big concept

Use it commonly for wishes, doubt, emotion, recommendations, and
non-established/desired situations.

``` text
MAIN CLAUSE + QUE + SUBJUNCTIVE
Quiero que estudies.       I want you to study.
Espero que estés bien.     I hope you are well.
Es importante que practiques.
Dudo que sea verdad.
```

Present subjunctive formation: 1. Start with **yo** form of present
indicative. 2. Drop **-o**. 3. Add opposite endings: - -AR → e, es, e,
emos, éis, en - -ER/-IR → a, as, a, amos, áis, an Examples: hablar →
hable; comer → coma; tener → tenga; hacer → haga; ir → vaya; ser → sea.

**Indicative vs subjunctive** - *Sé que viene.* (I know he comes/is
coming --- asserted fact) - *Espero que venga.* (I hope he comes ---
wish) - *Busco el libro que está aquí.* (known book) - *Busco un libro
que sea fácil.* (unspecified book with desired quality)

## Connectors for fluent speech

-   sequence: primero, luego, después, finalmente
-   reason: porque, ya que, debido a que
-   result: por eso, así que, por lo tanto
-   contrast: pero, aunque, sin embargo, mientras que
-   opinion: en mi opinión, creo que, desde mi punto de vista
-   examples: por ejemplo, es decir, en otras palabras

**B1 output mission:** tell connected stories, explain your opinion,
handle travel issues, discuss work/study, and keep a conversation going
with follow-up questions.

# 5. 🧠 B2: Confident & Nuanced Communication

Study and use: - Subjunctive in more contexts; imperfect subjunctive
(**hablara/hablase**, **comiera/comiese**). - Conditional sentences: *Si
tuviera tiempo, viajaría.* - Past perfect: **había + participle** (*Ya
había salido*). - Passive and **se** constructions: *Se habla español;
Se venden libros.* - Relative clauses: que, quien, cuyo, donde, lo
que. - Reported speech: *Dijo que estaba cansado.* - Register: casual,
neutral, formal; usted/ustedes choices. - Idioms, collocations,
phrasal-like expressions, discourse markers.

### B2 learning loop

``` text
NATIVE CONTENT
   ↓ listen/read once for gist
   ↓ listen/read again for details
   ↓ collect 3–5 useful chunks
   ↓ summarize in Spanish (spoken or written)
   ↓ verify / get correction
   ↓ reuse those chunks next week
```

**B2 output mission:** discuss news, society, technology, culture,
education, and abstract ideas; support opinions with reasons and
counterarguments.

# 6. 🎓 C1--C2: Advanced to Mastery

## C1 priorities

-   Understand long lectures, films, podcasts, and implied meaning.
-   Speak spontaneously with clear structure.
-   Write essays, reports, reviews, and professional messages.
-   Refine transitions, tone, idioms, and regional variation.
-   Recognize irony, understatement, humor, and cultural references.

## C2 priorities

-   Precision and stylistic control---not merely "more vocabulary."
-   Interpret ambiguity, wordplay, subtext, and rhetorical intent.
-   Adapt register for academic, literary, professional, and casual
    settings.
-   Produce polished arguments and creative writing.
-   Understand diverse accents and fast speech, including unfamiliar
    speakers.

### Advanced grammar checklist

-   [ ] All indicative tenses and aspect distinctions
-   [ ] Present, past, and perfect subjunctive
-   [ ] Imperfect subjunctive and sequence of tenses
-   [ ] Conditional clauses (real, hypothetical, counterfactual)
-   [ ] Relative clauses and pronoun placement
-   [ ] Passive voice and impersonal **se**
-   [ ] Ser/estar nuance; por/para nuance
-   [ ] Prepositions and verb + preposition patterns
-   [ ] Reported speech and register
-   [ ] Idiomatic collocations, discourse markers, regional forms

# 7. 🧩 The "Never Confuse These" Desk

## POR vs PARA (starter compass)

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  PARA                                POR
  ----------------------------------- -----------------------------------
  destination: *para Madrid*          movement through: *por el parque*

  purpose: *para estudiar*            reason/cause: *por amor*

  recipient: *para ti*                exchange: *por diez euros*

  deadline: *para mañana*             duration: *por dos horas*
                                      (regional/contextual)
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

This is a useful first map, not every exception.

## Saber vs conocer

-   **saber**: facts, information, how to do something --- *Sé la
    respuesta; sé nadar.*
-   **conocer**: familiarity with people/places --- *Conozco a María;
    conozco Madrid.*

## Muy vs mucho

-   **muy + adjective/adverb:** muy bueno, muy rápido
-   **mucho + noun / verb:** mucho dinero, estudio mucho
-   **mucho/a/os/as** agrees with noun: mucha agua, muchos libros.

## Desde / desde hace / hace

-   *Vivo aquí desde 2020.* (since 2020)
-   *Vivo aquí desde hace tres años.* (for three years, still living
    here)
-   *Llegué hace tres años.* (I arrived three years ago)

## Hay / está / están

-   *Hay una farmacia.* (existence)
-   *La farmacia está cerca.* (location of a specific item)
-   *Las llaves están en la mesa.* (specific plural items)

# 8. 📚 Vocabulary Mastery System

Learn words in **chunks and contexts**, not isolated lists.

``` text
WORD → CHUNK → PERSONAL SENTENCE → AUDIO → RECALL → REUSE
```

Example: - Word: **aprovechar** = make good use of - Chunk: **aprovechar
el tiempo** - Sentence: *Quiero aprovechar el tiempo para aprender
español.* - Recall tomorrow without looking; use it in a spoken answer.

### Vocabulary themes by stage

-   **A1:** identity, family, numbers, time, food, home, school, basic
    verbs.
-   **A2:** routine, health, shopping, travel, weather, hobbies, past
    experiences.
-   **B1:** work, study, relationships, plans, emotions, news, opinions.
-   **B2:** society, environment, technology, politics, culture,
    abstract concepts.
-   **C1--C2:** academic/professional terminology, idioms, literary
    language, nuance.

### Spaced review (simple schedule)

``` text
Learn today → review tomorrow → day 3 → day 7 → day 14 → day 30
```

Use flashcards for hard-to-recall items. Cards should include an example
sentence or audio where possible.

# 9. 🎧 Listening, Speaking, Reading, Writing

## Listening ladder

1.  Beginner slow audio with transcript.
2.  Familiar-topic videos with Spanish subtitles.
3.  Native podcasts with transcript or episode notes.
4.  Films/series: first Spanish subtitles, then no subtitles.
5.  Different accents: Spain, Mexico, Colombia, Argentina, Caribbean,
    etc.

**Method:** listen for gist → identify key phrases → replay → shadow
20--60 seconds → summarize aloud.

## Speaking ladder

``` text
A1: read sentences aloud
A2: answer daily questions for 1 minute
B1: voice note / monologue for 3–5 minutes
B2: conversation + opinion + follow-up questions
C1: debate, presentation, storytelling
C2: flexible spontaneous conversation with nuance
```

Shadowing: listen to one short phrase, pause, imitate rhythm and stress,
record yourself, compare. Prioritize intelligibility over trying to
erase your accent.

## Reading ladder

-   A1--A2: graded readers and short dialogues.
-   B1: short stories, learner news, accessible articles.
-   B2: novels, essays, Spanish-language journalism.
-   C1--C2: literature, specialized texts, satire, poetry, editorial
    writing.

## Writing ladder

-   A1: 5--8 simple sentences about yourself.
-   A2: routine / past weekend paragraph (80--120 words).
-   B1: narrative or opinion (150--250 words).
-   B2: structured argument (300--500 words).
-   C1: formal essay/report with nuance and style.
-   C2: polished writing for audience, purpose, and register.

Always revise: **meaning → grammar → vocabulary → naturalness →
accents/punctuation**.

# 14. 🏁 First 30 Days --- Start Here

## Week 1 --- Sounds & introductions

-   Alphabet, vowels, stress, greetings.
-   Pronouns, **ser**, basic nouns/articles.
-   Duolingo daily; read every sentence aloud.
-   Output: 30-second introduction.

## Week 2 --- Present-tense basics

-   Regular -ar/-er/-ir verbs; questions and negation.
-   Numbers, days, time, family.
-   Output: describe your family and daily routine in 8--10 sentences.

## Week 3 --- Everyday Spanish

-   **estar**, **hay**, adjective agreement, **tener**, **ir**.
-   Food, home, places, directions.
-   Output: restaurant and directions role-play.

## Week 4 --- Build and review

-   Stem-changers, gustar, reflexive routine verbs.
-   Review all mistakes from weeks 1--3.
-   Output: speak for 2 minutes; write 100 words; complete a beginner
    listening test.

**Day 30 checkpoint:** Can you introduce yourself, ask simple questions,
talk about routine, understand slow basic speech, and write a short
paragraph? If not, repeat/reinforce before rushing forward.

# 15. 🔥 Rules for Long-Term Mastery

1.  **Consistency beats intensity.** A manageable daily routine wins.
2.  **Understand before memorizing.** Ask what the grammar is doing.
3.  **Active recall beats rereading.** Close notes and produce Spanish.
4.  **Learn phrases, not only words.**
5.  **Speak from day one**, even if only to yourself.
6.  **Use comprehensible input:** content should be understandable
    enough to follow.
7.  **Correct recurring errors**, not every tiny mistake at once.
8.  **One dialect as your starting anchor is fine**; later expose
    yourself to many.
9.  **Do not translate every sentence word-for-word.** Build direct
    associations.
10. **CEFR levels are abilities, not app milestones.** Test real-world
    performance.
